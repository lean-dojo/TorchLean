# `TorchLean/Verify/RuntimeApprox`

Runtime approximation theorems bound forward and backward error under stated local operator
assumptions and tolerances. A runtime value is mapped into `ℝ` and compared with the real
specification. The graph theorems compose local bounds over typed graphs and have no MLP,
convolution or attention case, since an architecture is a composition of nodes. `NF` rounds with
`Flocq.round` after every primitive, and it is a backend we can reason about inside Lean. A
native path such as CUDA or LibTorch is covered only where a theorem connects its behavior to one of
these approximation relations.

- `Tolerance.lean`, `TorchLean/Verify/RuntimeApprox.lean`: tolerances (`ApproxTol`) and scalar and
  tensor approximation predicates.
- `ApproxTensor.lean`: shape-generic `ApproxTensor` and `linfNorm` lemmas that turn scalar
  absolute-error bounds into forward-error bounds on whole tensors.
- `Rounding.lean`: `ScalarApprox` and the bound for one rounded multiplication
  (`ScalarApprox.roundedMul`). Rounded addition uses FloatLib's `add_error` directly.
- `Constants.lean`: square roots of dimension counts that overflow their scalar representation.
- `Elementary.lean`, `InverseTrigonometric.lean`: correctly rounded elementary functions.
- `TorchLean/Verify/Floats/IEEE754/Expressions.lean`: binary-format `Expr` evaluation rounds each
  real operation (`FiniteEval`).
- `TorchLean/Verify/Floats/IEEE754/Reductions.lean`: a forward-error bound that holds for every
  valid reduction tree.
- `Graph/`: forward and reverse graph composition.
- `NF/`: `NF` operator bounds and their reverse-graph composition (`NF.backprop_approx`).
- `NF/Layers.lean`, `NF/MLP.lean`, `NF/CROWN.lean`: linear-layer, MLP and CROWN/IBP box bounds.
- `Roundoff.lean`, `Roundoff/Fold.lean`, `Roundoff/Mixed.lean`, `Roundoff/Rules.lean`,
  `Roundoff/Rules/`, `Roundoff/Routes.lean`, `Roundoff/Coverage.lean`: tensors in FloatLib's
  `roundoff`.
- `TorchLean/Verify/Coverage/`: analyses of registered operator and composite routes, and the bound
  theorems generated by successful analyses, by family.
- `Roundoff/Baseline.lean`: the comparison of the outcomes of those analyses with the outcomes
  recorded in `scripts/checks/roundoff_baseline.jsonl`.
- `Roundoff/`: `roundoff` bounds of operators, pullbacks, softmax, losses, convolution, pooling,
  composites, contractions and blocked online softmax attention.
- `OnlineSoftmax/Real.lean`: online softmax equals the two-pass value over `ℝ`, and the real
  algebra of its error bounds.
- `OnlineSoftmax.lean`: the rounding error of online softmax in every IEEE format.
- `OnlineSoftmax/Binary32.lean`: those bounds evaluated in `binary32` at 4096 and 16384 keys, and
  the tactic `softmax_bound` that evaluates them at any sizes.
- `Roundoff/SoftmaxReplay.lean`: softmax and its pullback, with their exact-rational replay
  branches.
- `Roundoff/SoftmaxReplay/Binary32.lean`: `binary32` inputs on which softmax takes its replay
  branches.
- `Roundoff/SoftmaxShift.lean`: the exponentials of stable softmax sum to at least one.
- `TransformerBlock.lean`: a per-token bound between `Arithmetic.block`, a rounded schedule of a
  pre-normalization transformer block, and the exact block. The executed op-table block rescales
  inside its layer normalization and adds each bias last, so the bound does not cover it.
- `TransformerBlock/Nearest.lean`: that bound under nearest rounding in any IEEE format.
  `TorchLeanExamples/Models/Sequence/GPT2BlockBound.lean` evaluates it at GPT-2 small in `binary32`.
- `TransformerBlock/Ops.lean`: its real `layerNorm`, `softmax`, `attention`, `heads` and `block` as
  forwards of op-table operations.
- `OptimizerContract.lean`: `OptimizerContract` and `runApprox_of_stepStreamApprox` over any finite
  gradient stream.
- `Roundoff/Recovery.lean`: sigmoid, tanh and power slopes enclosed in certified rational intervals.
- `RL/`: RL backups in executable IEEE semantics, runtime-checked finiteness, and the PPO ratio.

Import the module of the theorem you use, for example the end-to-end `NF` bounds:

```lean
import TorchLean.Verify.RuntimeApprox.NF.EndToEnd
```

## Tensors In `roundoff`

`Roundoff.lean` registers TorchLean's tensors with FloatLib's `roundoff` analysis. It reads a
tensor entry at a coordinate and carries a step bound through tensor folds and index loops
(`Roundoff/Fold.lean`). General operators go through the coordinate lemmas registered in
`Roundoff/Rules/`: `Tensor`, `Movement`, `Broadcast`, `Reduction`, `ReduceOrder`, `Ordering`,
`Elementwise`,
`Power`, `Literal`, `Products`, `Composite`, `NormalizedRows`, `Softmax`, `OnlineSoftmax`,
`Convolution` and `Autograd`. `Roundoff/Rules/OnlineSoftmax.lean` registers the hand bounds of
`OnlineSoftmax.lean` for the online softmax iterators. `Roundoff/Rules/Autograd.lean` registers the
compiler replacements the autograd proofs prove (`eval` is `evalWithArray`, `backpropCtx` is
`backpropCtxSaved`), so the analysis reads indexed forward and saved reverse execution through
their source programs.

`Roundoff/Rules/ReduceOrder.lean` covers reductions in an order other than the left fold
(`Tensor.ReduceOrder`). It reads an entry of `Tensor.reduceIn order` for a sum, product, maximum or
minimum and of the batched matrix product `Tensor.matmulCommonBatchIn order` as the order's fold,
and bounds a finite sum in every format of the configured binary family on an IEEE descriptor by
`((1 + u) ^ d - 1)` times the sum of the absolute values, absolute and mixed, with `d` the depth of
the order's tree: `⌈log₂ (n + 1)⌉` for `pairwise`, the depth of the blocks for `blocked w`, and for
a given tree under the premise that it covers the count. The import of a native Float32 sum in an
order is the configured sum of the imports when that sum is finite, so the native backend gets the
same bound. On finite entries a maximum or minimum in such an order has the real value of the left
fold, whose analysis applies; the stored values can differ in the sign of a zero. The coverage
modules analyze `reduce`, `mean`, `contract`, `matmul`, `scaledMatmul`, `pool`, `cumsum`, `cumprod`,
`cummax` and `cummin` in the orders `pairwise`, `blocked ?width` and `ofTree ?tree` as well as in
the left fold, and the forwards of the sums, means, contractions, matrix products, sum and average
pools and cumulative sums in those orders carry the depth. So does the forward of a scaled matrix
product there: an entry of `Attention.scaledProductsIn` is `Attention.scaledProductIn` on the batch
slices (`Attention.scaledProductsIn_apply`), the computed value of its checked fold is the order's
sum of the products (`checkedAdd_fold_fst`, in `Rules/Products.lean`), and its replay is the left
fold's. Its pullbacks and tangent are the left fold's in every order, so their rows in an order have
the default rows' evidence. A finite product of `n` entries in such an order rounds `n` times, as
the left fold does, so it has the left fold's relative error `(1 + u) ^ n - 1` in every order and an
absolute error from underflow that the order's tree accumulates (`productBudget`); the native
Float32 product gets the same bound, and the forwards of a product reduction and of a cumulative
product in those orders carry it. A running maximum or minimum in such an order is the input at the
index the order selects (`Tensor.cummaxIn_fst_apply`, `Tensor.cumminIn_fst_apply`), with the input's
error there; at a configured binary format that index is the left fold's
(`ConfiguredBinary.cummaxIn_eq`, `ConfiguredBinary.cumminIn_eq`). The rounded FP32 backend has no
rule for these folds, as for the left fold.

Layer normalization, batch normalization over batch statistics and group normalization divide the
centered values by a detached scale before squaring them. `Roundoff/Rules/Composite.lean` bounds the
error of the scale of the first two, `Tensor.normalizationScale`, by the largest error among the
centered values and `sqrt |epsilon|`, absolute and mixed. It also reads a statistic broadcast back
along the kept axes at its reduced shape, and rewrites group normalization by
`TorchLean.Tensor.groupNorm_def`, which types the statistics of the grouped view at that shape. The
scale that `Normalization.normalizedRows` casts to the shape of the rows, in the JVP and backward
kernels of layer and batch normalization, is read at the reduced shape too. We register Mathlib's
`Equiv.apply_symm_apply`, so in the sums of `Normalization.normalizedRows` along a row, a statistic
broadcast back along the row is read at the coordinate of the row. The largest magnitude in the
scale of group normalization is exact on finite entries.

`Roundoff/Rules/NormalizedRows.lean` bounds each normalized value and each inverse standard
deviation of `Normalization.normalizedRows` as one function of its rows: from rows whose entries
have mixed errors, `roundoff` infers a mixed bound on each. Mixed analysis applies these bounds in
the JVP and backward kernels of layer and batch normalization, and absolute analysis reads the same
entries through the definition of `Normalization.normalizedRows`.
`TorchLean/Verify/Coverage/Layers/LayerNorm.lean`, which analyzes these kernels, is the one coverage
module that imports these rules.

`Roundoff/Coverage.lean` defines `#roundoff_coverage`, which analyzes the routes that
`Roundoff/Routes.lean` reads from the code of a declaration. The modules under
`TorchLean/Verify/Coverage/` run it on every registered operator, tape constructor and tensor
composite, family by family, and declare one theorem for each new statement. When a route has the
same statement as a theorem already available in the module or its imports, the coverage pass
records and reuses that theorem instead of declaring a duplicate. It treats propagation theorems
the same way. Composite proofs apply the reused theorems and record the primitive and propagation
results on which they depend. A module whose routes or propagation theorems repeat those of another
imports it, as the operators of the table import the tensor functions they apply.
A theorem for the configured scalar holds over an arbitrary format and takes the hypothesis that
the format is IEEE, or the scalar's storage, only where its proof uses it, so one without that
hypothesis also holds at OCP's E4M3, whose encoding is not IEEE. An entry `every M` registers every
public function of the modules `M` and under it whose value is the scalar or a tensor and that no
earlier coverage records. `Coverage/Training.lean` registers the functions of `TorchLean/NN/RL/`
and `TorchLean/NN/Optimizer/` this way, with one entry each:

```lean
#roundoff_coverage
  reinforcement := every TorchLean.NN.RL
  optimizer := every TorchLean.NN.Optimizer
```

FloatLib's `#roundoff_check` evaluates a mixed theorem at given inputs and prints the computed
value, the exact or enclosed reference and the bound:

```lean
import TorchLean.Verify.Coverage.Training
import FloatLib.Floats.Formats.BinaryInterchange.Transcendentals.CorrectlyRounded.Runtime
open FloatLib.Floats TorchLean.Coverage.Training

#roundoff_check RL.tdResidual.value_mixedError (value := (1.5 : Binary32))
  (reward := (0.1 : Binary32)) (gamma := (0.99 : Binary32)) (nextValue := (1.4141414 : Binary32))
  (done := false)
```

It found two limits of these theorems that no order of the operations removes, each at the inputs
it names only. `RL.tdResidual` computes `r + γ V' - V`, which is ill-conditioned where `r + γ V'`
is close to `V`: above it computes `0` against the residual `1.7360605e-8` of its inputs, within
the bound `1.729e-7`, which then does not fix the sign (at `r = 0.5`, `γ = 0.5` and `V' = 4` it
computes `1` with the bound `3.278e-7`). A result correct to the rounding needs error-free
transformations. A term of `RL.PolicyGradient.weightedExpm1x` below half the least subnormal rounds
to `0`: at `l = -110`, `s = 1/2`, `x = -109.5` and `quarter = 1/8` the term is about `2.51e-49` and
the bound `8.049e-46` is the underflow term, while at `l = -10` the bound is `1.207e-12` against a
term of `6.75e-6`.
The coverage check in `scripts/checks/Library.lean`, which `scripts/lake.sh lint` runs, fails unless
every public operator specification and tape constructor under `TorchLean/` is registered with
`#roundoff_coverage`, every operator of the table with a rule has its equation lemma and, when one
of its ports holds the scalar, a route analyzed for the configured scalar, and every `op_cert`
certificate speaks about a route of its operator. It also compares the outcomes of the analyses
with `scripts/checks/roundoff_baseline.jsonl`, as `Roundoff/Baseline.lean` defines. The file
records for each analysis its status, a hash of its reference, the budgets `r` and `a` or `ε` of
its bound with their values at binary16, bfloat16, binary32 and binary64, and its premises and
caveats. The check fails when an analysis gets a lower status, a budget larger at one of these
formats, a changed budget that has no values, a changed reference, or, at the same status, a new
premise or caveat, and when an analysis is gone. It also fails on a new analysis and on an
improvement, until
`TORCHLEAN_ROUNDOFF_BASELINE=update scripts/lake.sh env lean scripts/checks/Library.lean`
rewrites the file. That command refuses while there are regressions, and
`TORCHLEAN_ROUNDOFF_BASELINE=accept` writes them too and prints each one. The guide's
[operator chapter](https://lean-dojo.github.io/TorchLean/blueprint/Semantics-and-Graphs/The-Operator-Table/)
lists the operators of the table by family, with their CUDA entries, certificates and bound
propagations.

Registration records an analysis attempt; it does not guarantee a bound theorem. 88 of the 3649
analyses fail and supply no numerical bound theorem:
- 50, all for the configured scalar, stop at an integer computed from tensor values: the exponent
  of `Op.frexp` [forward 1] and the tie counts of the max and min gradients and tangents of
  `Op.reduce` and `Op.scatter`. Treating those counts as fixed index data would hide a change in
  gradient selection when rounding changes a tie.
- 17 for the configured scalar exceed the analyzer's default heartbeat budget: `Op.attention`
  [online backward 2] and [twoPass backward 2], `Op.batchNormRunning` [backward 1] and
  [backward 2], and `Op.multiheadAttention` [forward] and [jvp] in both modes, and `Op.gelu`
  [tanh backward], `Op.attentionFromScores` [backward 1] and [prepared backward 1] and
  `Op.distill` [backward 0] and [jvp] in mixed mode.
- 4 for the other scalars exceed it too: `Op.batchNormRunning` [backward 1] and [backward 2] for
  native `Float32` and for `FP32`.
- 17 are of the functions of `TorchLean/NN/RL/`. The two routes of `RL.DQN.loss`, which casts its
  input along `BatchInput.type_eq`, stop at that `Eq.ndrec` in both modes for the configured scalar
  and for native `Float32`, and `RL.Tabular.qLearningTarget` stops at its `match` on the action
  count in absolute analysis. `RL.PolicyGradient.kl`, `klFromLogits` and `klTilted` exceed the
  heartbeat budget in both modes for the configured scalar, and `RL.PPO.loss`,
  `RL.PolicyGradient.actorCriticLoss`, `RL.PolicyGradient.entropy` and
  `RL.Tabular.qLearningTarget` in mixed mode for the configured scalar.

## `NF` And `FP32`

The `NF` rounded-real bounds for the sigmoid are built on `divPosErrorBound`, and
`reciprocalSigmoidBoundScalar_le_one` bounds the budget of the sequence `1 / (1 + exp (-x))` by
`1` under small-error hypotheses. The row mean divides by the exact count with one rounding
(`Context.divNat`), so `NF.approx_meanRow` needs no denominator margin.
`approxTensor_reluTwoLayerMLP` and `reluTwoLayerMLP_ibp_sound` bound a rounded two-layer
ReLU MLP and its IBP output box in any `NF β fexp rnd` with round-to-nearest. `FP32` is the instance
at binary32 precision, a rounded-real model distinct from Lean's `Float32`.

## Numerical Certificates

`TorchLean/Verify/NumericalCertificate.lean` and `TorchLean/Verify/NumericalCertificate/` check
interval certificates for graphs of registered operators, with FloatLib's outward-rounded binary32
intervals and the selected kernel capsules. `check` does not
trust a certificate's node ranges. It checks that the source endpoints are finite and ordered,
replans under the backend profile and rebuilds the range trace from the graph and the sources. Every
rebuilt enclosure must be finite and ordered, and the result must match the certificate. Success
gives a `NumericalCertificate.Checked`. Enclosure of the exact-real graph is the separate
`NumericalCertificate.RealEnclosure` used by `CheckedExecution.errorTrace`. An unsupported operation
fails with its node id and operation name rather than receiving `[-inf,+inf]`.

`deriveNodeRange` admits a node whose transfer folds a reduction from the left only when the first
selected kernel with the node's id declares the policy `fixed .leftFold`
(`TorchLean/Verify/NumericalCertificate/NumericalPolicy.lean`). It establishes the consumer's policy
gate, not the numerical correctness of a native implementation. A reduction, mean, matrix product
or pool in an order other than the left fold has no transfer.
